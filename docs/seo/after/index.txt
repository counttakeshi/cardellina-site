Originally from the UK and a birder across Latin America for years, Ben has guided in Costa Rica and now calls the Chiapas highlands home. He's a fluent Spanish speaker with a sharp eye for a rarity, a patient teacher, and easy company through a long day in the field.

Why "Cardellina"
Four of the five live here
Cardellina is a genus of five warblers, and four of them occur in Chiapas,
including the Pink-headed Warbler, which is found almost nowhere else. The three on our
logo reflect our team.
Pink-headed Warbler C. versicolor A resident of the Chiapas highlands, found almost nowhere else. Valente , born and raised here.
Red-faced Warbler C. rubrifrons A migrant from the north that winters in these mountains. Ben , who arrived from further afield and settled here.
Red Warbler C. rubra A Mexican endemic of the highlands further north, and the one of the three you won't find in Chiapas. Representing Mexico as a whole and our plan to extend our tours throughout the country.
The remaining two, Wilson's and Canada Warbler, pass through Chiapas on migration each
year.
In the field
How we like to bird
A few things matter to us beyond the birds themselves, mainly how we treat the places we
visit, the people who live there, and our guests.
Ethical field craft, meaning careful use of playback and a respectful distance from nesting birds
We seek out local guides and families doing good work, and build them into our trips
As friends ourselves, we want our guests to feel like one of the gang, to have a birding-with-friends feel
Free birding workshops where we can, to encourage local interest in conservation
Constant scouting, so you see birds few visitors reach
